We scored 100 TinyStories documents (74,760 bytes, budget 32 patches) and 30 WikiText documents (122,880 bytes, 64 patches) with the seed-0 models, on a CPU (\texttt{docs/error\_analysis\_part3.md}, generated by \texttt{scripts/error\_analysis\_part3.py}). The documents are the first to pass a length filter; the tables describe one seed and are not tests.

\textbf{Quantitative.} Table~\ref{tab:terc} apportions the extra loss by the entropy tercile of the patch that holds the byte. Every rule suffers most on the high-entropy third, where the full-cache loss is also greatest, but keeping high-entropy patches does not shield those bytes (+0.0392 against +0.0225 for recency on TinyStories; +0.0190 against +0.0062 on WikiText), and it penalizes the easy bytes besides (+0.0194 against +0.0080 and +0.0152 against +0.0023 on the lowest third, which holds 57\% and 55\% of the bytes). The entropy rule retains almost nothing but high-entropy patches (44.6\% and 18.1\% of the highest third survive, none of the lowest), and they are short: the mean kept patch is 1.15 and 1.13 bytes long and 98.0\% and 99.7\% of them have one or two bytes, against 4.46 and 4.47 bytes for recency. This is a confound of length (a surprising byte often forms a patch of its own, so that a mean taken over a short patch runs high). It does not account for the whole result, since the sum rule, which favours long patches, ties random on TinyStories and loses on WikiText (Section~\ref{sec:neg}). By byte class, the entropy rule loses most on word-initial lowercase letters (+0.0437 against recency on WikiText) and on capitals in TinyStories (+0.1426), and gains a little on digits and capitals in WikiText ($-0.0199$ and $-0.0083$). The damage is concentrated: the largest 1\% of bytes carry 30.4\% (TinyStories) and 18.0\% (WikiText) of the extra loss against recency, and entropy is worse than recency on 40.7\% and 51.0\% of bytes and better on 33.1\% and 42.4\%.

\textbf{Why? Attention.} Figure~S1 of the supplement (\texttt{report/supplement.pdf}) shows the Spearman correlation between the entropy of an old patch (at least 8 patches back) and the attention it receives. It is negative in the middle and upper layers of most models: in all six layers of all three TinyStories seeds ($-0.04$ to $-0.55$), and in layers 2 to 5 of the three prose-only WikiText seeds ($-0.007$ to $-0.39$). In the seed-0 TinyStories model the highest-entropy third of old patches receives about half the attention of the lowest third in layers 1 and 2. The model, it seems, reads predictable patches more than surprising ones, and surprising bytes are dealt with by the local windows. This is a correlation, not an intervention, and the WikiText code-mix seed 0 model shows the opposite sign in most layers while its eviction result keeps the same sign, so that the account is incomplete.



\textbf{Qualitative.} In the examples \texttt{|} marks a patch boundary; all are genuine output.

\emph{Example 1, TinyStories document 0 (1,201 bytes, 4.0 bytes per patch).} Boundaries fall before surprising letters and dissect names: \texttt{R|o|xy. |Ro|x|y |loved to |c|limb.}, while predictable words remain whole patches (\texttt{She |had |never |seen |anything |}). At the last patch the caches (budget 32) hold, for recency, \texttt{played |together |all the |time. |And |Ro|x|y |l|earned that |...} (BPB 0.744, $+0.010$ over full); for keep high entropy, \texttt{\ |s|f|\ |B|s|s|Ro|\ |b|f|b|f|a|s|a|i|...} (0.751, $+0.017$), a rosary of one-letter fragments that keeps \texttt{Ro} but not \texttt{Roxy}; for keep low entropy, \texttt{Once upon a time|, in a |forest, there li|...|never |seen |ried |again, |...} (0.743, $+0.009$), whole words from the opening of the story.

\emph{Example 2, WikiText document 0 (4,096 bytes, budget 64).} The entropy cache at the last patch is \texttt{o|Ho|E|A|O|S|A|a|6|\ |a\ |e|5\ |1|e|e|1|...}: capitals, digits and vowels, with not a word among them. BPB is 1.752 ($+0.033$) against 1.725 for recency ($+0.006$), whose cache holds the section on distribution (\texttt{Norway |to |the |A|zores |and |Mo|r|occo}).

\emph{Example 3, a locally predictable byte.} In TinyStories document 56 the byte \texttt{d} in \texttt{They argue[d]} costs 0.02 bits with the full cache, 0.01 with recency and 5.97 with keep high entropy. The recent bytes are present (the newest 8 patches are spared), so what changes is the older cache; we conjecture that a cache of fragments distorts the global state that feeds the decoder even for easy bytes, but we did not test it. A WikiText instance: \texttt{to the northeast , i[s]} costs 0.84, 0.86 and 4.15 bits.

\emph{Example 4, where entropy helps.} In TinyStories document 13 the byte \texttt{S} in \texttt{All the animals cheered for [S]am} costs 4.04 bits with the full cache, 7.35 with recency and 3.46 with keep high entropy. The name Sam occurs several times earlier in the story. We did not verify whether the entropy cache held a patch containing the name. Such cases exist but are outweighed on average.

\emph{Example 5, generations that part company.} We continued the same prompt greedily with the full cache and with 32 (TinyStories) or 64 (WikiText) patches (\texttt{results/part3/generation\_divergence.md}). On TinyStories document 0 the full cache continues \texttt{find your friends."  Roxy was so happy to help Roxy.}; recency diverges at byte 17 (\texttt{find your friends to climb the leaves.}) and random and keep low entropy at byte 10 (\texttt{find your home and they would...}); keep high entropy remains with the full-cache text until byte 25 (\texttt{Roxyy said, "Thank you, Billy.}). On WikiText document 1 recency stays with the full cache until byte 65, whereas random and keep high entropy diverge at the first byte (\texttt{r the 1970 [dash] 1996 season}). Greedy text from models so small is repetitive (\texttt{the state of the state}), so we read these as evidence of where the compressed cache alters the prediction, not as measures of quality: on these four prompts no policy reproduces the true continuation better than the full cache by more than a few bytes, and the examples are anecdotal.
